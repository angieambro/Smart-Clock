# Hardware Notes

This document follows the Talkie Toaster phased hardware plan. Keep the wiring and `shared/pins.h` in agreement.

## Power Rails

- **Phase 1:** Power the Due from the 5 V power bank through its Micro-USB port.
  The barrel jack requires 7-12 V; 5 V from a power bank will not power it.
  The TFT, DS3231, DHT11, encoder, and indicator LEDs can be tested from the
  Due 3V3 rail as specified for the bench test. A low-current power bank may
  shut off unless its low-current mode is enabled.
- **Phase 2:** Split external battery/power-bank power before the Due so it
  feeds the Due and the Joy-It SBC-Buck01 (LM2596S) in parallel. Adjust and
  verify the converter at 3.3 V before connecting external loads; connect
  grounds in common. Do not connect the converter output to the Due 3V3 pin.
- Never power the DC motor, SG90 servo, or DFPlayer speaker from the Due's
  3V3/5V headers. Keep those loads disconnected during Phase 1. Do not use an
  LM317 linear regulator for the motor loads.
- Due signal pins are 3.3 V only. They are not 5 V tolerant.
- The Due and DS3231 modules have I2C pull-ups; do not add external I2C
  pull-up resistors.

## Pin Map

| Component signal | Arduino Due connection |
| --- | --- |
| LEDs 1-6 | D1-D6, each through its 220 ohm resistor |
| DHT11 data | D7; Phase 1 sensor power comes from Due 3V3, with the shown 10 kohm pull-up |
| SG90 servo signal | D8; servo power must come from an appropriate external rail |
| Motor transistor base | D9 through the 1 kohm resistor |
| RTC SDA/SCL | Due SDA1/SCL1 (Wire1) |
| DFPlayer RX/TX | Due TX1/RX1 (D18/D19), crossed TX-to-RX |
| TFT reset/DC/CS/backlight | D22/D24/D26/D28 |
| Encoder A/B/push | D30/D32/D34 |
| TFT SCL/SDA | Due ICSP SCK/MOSI (hardware SPI clock and data; TFT SCL is SPI clock and TFT SDA is MOSI) |

The selected display listing specifies a 1.8-inch, 128x160 SPI TFT using an ST7735S controller. The project uses Adafruit_ST7735 for its graphics driver. The module's encoder and display are separate devices on the same board: encoder A/B/button are GPIO inputs, while the screen uses SPI.

D1 is not a hardware-PWM output on the Due, so its LED may switch rather than
dim smoothly with the other sun-lamp LEDs. D1 is also the programming-port TX;
disconnect that LED if it interferes with uploads.

## Motor Driver

The NPN transistor is a low-side switch: motor positive connects to a verified
external motor supply, motor negative to the transistor collector, and emitter
to common ground. D9 drives the base through 1 kohm. The code uses PWM on D9
with an initial duty of 200/255. The D8 servo signal is generated by the Servo
library; supply the servo from an appropriate external rail and share ground.

The flyback diode goes across the motor, with its cathode (striped end) toward 3.3 V and its anode toward the transistor collector. Choose a diode and transistor rated for the motor's measured startup/stall current. Do not rely on the transistor voltage drop alone to protect the motor from overvoltage; check the motor voltage and current under load.

## Audio and Display

- Put audio on a FAT32 DFPlayer microSD card under `/mp3/`: 0001 alarm,
  0002 timer, 0003 high temperature, and 0004 low temperature. The firmware
  starts at volume 10 out of 30. Keep the DFPlayer and speaker disconnected
  during Phase 1.
- Connect the speaker between DFPlayer `SPK1` and `SPK2`. These are bridged outputs; neither speaker lead goes to ground.
- TFT reset/DC/CS use D22/D24/D26. TFT SCL/SDA are SPI SCK/MOSI on the Due ICSP header.
- The Due cannot hardware-PWM D28. Firmware therefore sets the backlight pin on continuously. For brightness control, move the BLK wire to an available Due PWM pin and update `TFT_BACKLIGHT_PIN`.
- The current bench test reads the DHT11 on D7 every two seconds and displays
  temperature and humidity on the ST7735 TFT. The RTC and DFPlayer are not
  initialized by this test.

## Due Pin Cautions

- D1 is also the Due programming-port UART TX pin. The firmware uses
  `SerialUSB` for debugging, but the LED connected to D1 can still load the
  programming UART during upload. If uploads fail, disconnect LED1 while
  uploading and check the wiring.
- The Due GPIO signals are 3.3 V. Do not feed a 5 V signal into a Due input.
- The firmware's debug messages use the Due's native USB port. Select that port in the serial monitor after uploading; the programming USB port is used for upload.
